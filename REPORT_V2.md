# Phase 2 — multi-modal extension and blinded recovery tests

This extends `REPORT.md` (phase 1, Qp from transcriptomics on GSE30321). Every number here is in
`results/metrics_v2.csv` or the file named beside it, and is regenerated by `python src/run_v2.py`.
Intervals are 95% cluster-bootstrap intervals over the held-out unit unless stated otherwise.

**Read `figures/fig0_architecture_status.png` first.** It is the architecture diagram with every box
marked as tested with positive evidence, tested with a weak or null result, or requiring partner data.

## Bottom line

1. **No sample in any public dataset used here has more than one modality with an outcome.** Every
   metric below belongs to one modality. Nothing in this work is a fused multi-omic prediction.
2. **Two boxes have positive predictive evidence, both from transcriptomics with whole experiments
   held out:** high vs low Qp (AUC 0.92, 0.80–0.99; phase 1) and high vs low viability
   (AUC 0.84, 0.70–0.94).
3. **The BMS metabolomics paper (Yao et al. 2021) published no per-clone data.** The cross-library
   prediction could not be run. On the published summary statistics, the pre-registered pathway
   ranking recovers the TCA cycle (rank 6 of 53, p = 0.02) but places alanine/aspartate/glutamate
   metabolism only 15th; the joint test gives p = 0.06.
4. **Knockout ranking recovers validated targets at chance** (AUC 0.50; 2 of 14 testable targets in
   the top 100, p = 0.19). The FBA route is uninformative.
5. **The pre-registered pathway-alignment test between transcripts and proteins is null**
   (ρ = 0.16, p = 0.13). Genes do agree weakly across the two modalities (ρ = 0.18, p < 0.0001),
   and pathway-level agreement appears in a sensitivity run without the temperature shift (ρ = 0.34).
6. **Latent dynamics, glycans, scale risk and any fused prediction require partner data.**

## P1. Metabolomics and qP on the BMS paper (Yao et al. 2021, *Metabolites* 11:823)

**What is public.** The data availability statement reads "Not applicable". The supplement is one
PDF. It holds Table S3 (Pearson correlation of each annotated metabolite with qP and with peak VCD
across the 12 clones of library 1), the list of 34 targeted metabolites without values, and figures.
There is no per-clone metabolite table for either library.

**Cross-library prediction (library 1 → library 2): not run.** It needs per-clone values. The only
per-clone values shown are scatter plots of the two metabolites the authors selected after their own
analysis; a "prediction" from those would be circular. The heatmap has about two pixels per
metabolite row and was not digitised. No data were fabricated or guessed.

**Pathway recovery on Table S3 (pre-registered before computing; `figures/fig4_metabolomics_qp.png`).**
92 metabolites, all mapped to KEGG; 53 KEGG metabolic pathways with at least three measured members.
Statistic: mean Fisher-z correlation with qP inside the pathway minus outside it. Null: 10,000
permutations of metabolite labels.

| Target pathway | Rank of 53 (Δ statistic) | Permutation p | Rank by the authors' own over-representation statistic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Citrate cycle (TCA cycle) | 6 | 0.020 | 1 |
| Alanine, aspartate and glutamate metabolism | 15 | 0.050 | 7 |
| Both: mean rank 10.5 vs 27.2 expected | — | 0.061 | — |

Ranks 1–5 went to pathways with three or four measured metabolites each (terpenoid backbone
biosynthesis, butanoate metabolism, C5-branched dibasic acid metabolism, nicotinate metabolism,
glutathione metabolism).

**Reading.** The TCA cycle is recovered. Alanine/aspartate/glutamate metabolism is in the top third,
not at the top, and the joint test does not reach 0.05. The ranking has little contrast to work
with: 97% of the metabolites correlate positively with qP. This is a concordance check on the
authors' own summary statistics, made by someone who had read their conclusion; it is not an
independent discovery and not a prediction.

A post hoc run restricted to pathways annotated in Chinese hamster gives ranks 6 and 13 of 38 and
joint p = 0.10. It is not used to rescue the result.

## P2. Knockout ranking with blinded literature recovery (`figures/fig5_knockout_recovery.png`)

**Protocol.** Two rankings were built and committed (`de3fe41`) before any ground truth was opened.

- *Route A, data-driven (transcriptomics, GSE30321):* gene-level Δ between the top and bottom Qp
  quartiles, within experiment. Most negative Δ = knockout candidate; most positive = overexpression
  candidate. 1,707 genes.
- *Route B, mechanistic (iCHO1766, COBRApy FBA):* single-gene deletion for all 1,766 model genes;
  growth and IgG-synthesis capacity relative to wild type.

**Ground truth, collected afterwards.** Schulz et al. 2026 (*mAbs*; systematic review of 164
publications), supplementary sheet B: 60 genes whose knockout or knockdown raised titer or qP, 153
genes whose overexpression did. From Marzluf et al. 2026 only the genes named in the text could be
used, because the 36 retained knockouts are not published by name.

**Coverage.** 14 of the 60 validated knockout targets are on the array; 17 are in the model. BAX,
BAK1, BOK, BCKDHA/B, COSMC, NEU1 and NEU3 are not on the array. BOK is in neither, so the named true
negative could not be tested.

| Test | Result | Chance | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Route A, knockout end: AUC for 14 validated targets | 0.50 | 0.50 (null range 0.35–0.65) | 0.50 |
| Route A, knockout end: validated targets in the top 100 | 2 (GLUL rank 29, DAG1 rank 30) | 0.8 | 0.19 |
| Route A, overexpression end: AUC for 37 validated targets | 0.39 | 0.50 (0.40–0.59) | 0.99 |
| Route A with the between-experiment Δ: validated targets in the top 100 | 0 | 0.8 | 1.00 |
| Route B, trade-off score: AUC for 17 validated targets | 0.49 | 0.50 (0.49–0.55) | 1.00 |
| Route B, tolerance call: validated targets called tolerated | 94% | 93% of all genes | — |

Named genes that could be ranked by Route A: GLUL 29, DAG1 30, LDHA 182, NID1 192, B4GALT1 367,
NEU2 856, FUT8 1,197, MYC 1,700 (of 1,707). With k = 50 the same two hits give p = 0.06.

**Reading.** Recovery is at chance on both routes. With 14 testable positives the test could only
have detected a strong effect, so this is no evidence of recovery, not proof that the approach
cannot work. FBA calls GAPDH, ALDOA, PGK1 and LDHA knockouts harmless, where the Marzluf screen found
them lethal or selected against; the model's isozyme rules absorb single deletions. One call matches
known biology: deleting FUT8 removes the model's capacity to make its core-fucosylated IgG.

## P3. Other endpoints on GSE30321 (`figures/fig6_endpoints.png`)

Modality: transcriptomics. Pipeline unchanged from phase 1. PLS and logistic regression on 1,000
probesets; top vs bottom tercile for AUC. "Temperature only" is a model given the culture
temperature and nothing else, under the same split.

| Endpoint | Arrays / experiments | AUC, experiments held out | AUC, families held out | R², experiments held out | R², temperature only |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Viability | 235 / 28 | 0.84 (0.70–0.94) | 0.77 (0.60–0.90) | 0.09 (−0.84–0.26) | 0.01 |
| Growth rate | 223 / 29 | 0.98 (0.95–1.00) | 0.99 (0.97–1.00) | 0.76 (0.68–0.83) | 0.62 |
| Viable cell density | 270 / 33 | 0.82 (0.68–0.96) | 0.73 (0.56–0.92) | 0.08 (−0.42–0.41) | 0.17 |
| q-lactate | 127 / 14 | 0.97 (0.90–1.00) | 0.94 (0.85–1.00) | 0.62 (0.46–0.76) | 0.61 |
| q-ammonium | 143 / 16 | 0.93 (0.85–1.00) | 0.95 (0.87–1.00) | 0.42 (0.15–0.57) | 0.35 |

TEMP_AUC_SENTENCE

Culture-grouped numbers, for contrast only: viability R² 0.77, density 0.77, against 0.09 and 0.08
with experiments held out. The leak found in phase 1 is larger for these endpoints than it was for Qp.

**Reading.**

- **Viability** can be separated into high and low from expression on unseen experiments, and the
  culture temperature alone cannot do it. A number cannot be predicted: R² is 0.09.
- **Growth rate and q-lactate** are predicted well, but temperature alone gets most of the way
  (R² 0.62 and 0.61). Expression adds a little for growth rate and nothing for q-lactate.
- **Viable cell density** behaves like viability: some high-vs-low separation, no regression skill.
- For high-vs-low AUC the pathway encoder is at or below raw genes on every endpoint (for example
  viability 0.79 vs 0.84). For regression the two are within each other's intervals
  (`results/metrics_endpoints.csv`).

## P4. Cross-modality alignment on a pathway basis (`figures/fig7_modality_alignment.png`)

Design pre-registered in commit `83b8d9e`. Each contrast is computed inside one modality; the test
is whether the pathway-level contrast vectors correlate.

**Leg 1, growth phase: transcriptomics (GSE30321) vs proteomics (PRIDE PXD053511).** Proteomics:
4 exponential vs 4 stationary samples, 4,207 proteins quantified, 87% mapped to a gene, 3,303 genes.
Transcriptomics: within-experiment Δ for growth rate. 557 genes are measured in both.

| Comparison | n | Spearman | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-registered primary**, iCHO1766 subsystems | 18 | 0.20 | 0.24 |
| **Pre-registered primary**, KEGG hamster pathways | 266 | 0.16 | 0.13 |
| Gene level, no pathway basis | 557 | 0.18 | < 0.0001 |
| Sensitivity (37 °C arrays only), iCHO1766 subsystems | 18 | 0.36 | 0.08 |
| Sensitivity (37 °C arrays only), KEGG hamster pathways | 266 | 0.34 | < 0.0001 |

**Leg 2, Qp association: transcriptomics vs metabolomics (Yao Table S3).** Spearman −0.12 over 10
iCHO1766 subsystems (p = 0.63) and −0.08 over 40 KEGG pathways (p = 0.66). Null.

**Reading.** The pre-registered pathway test is a null on both bases. Transcript and protein
contrasts do agree, weakly, gene by gene, and the pathway projection did not strengthen that. The
sensitivity run removes the main design mismatch (slow growth in GSE30321 mostly coincides with the
shift to 31 °C; the proteomics study has no shift) and then shows clear pathway-level agreement on
KEGG. It was registered as a sensitivity run, with that reason, and is reported as one. The iCHO1766
basis could not be tested properly: it covers metabolism only, the array carries 1,707 genes, and
only 18 subsystems reach five genes in both modalities.

Not run: the hamster proteome atlas (no contrast shared with another dataset here) and the one
Chinese hamster study on Metabolomics Workbench (no time).

## P5. Scale risk

- GSE30321 deposits no scale or format variable, so a small-to-large degradation test is impossible.
- Gao et al. 2016 (hypoxia on scale-up to 5,000 L) is closed access with no deposited data found.
- **Substitute, a format contrast and not a scale contrast:** GSE104787, one IgG-producing line in
  3 shake flasks and 3 bioreactors, 18 arrays. Pre-registered question: does HALLMARK_HYPOXIA lead
  the bioreactor-vs-flask Δ? It ranks **17 of 50** (p = 0.012 against random gene sets, in a
  contrast where 16 of 50 sets pass 0.05). Proliferation programmes lead (E2F targets, MYC targets,
  G2M checkpoint). Sampling day explains a median 24.6% of expression variance and culture system
  2.1%, which matches the deposit's own summary.
- The scale-risk box stays "requires partner data".

## P6. Glycans

Stopped inside the 90-minute timebox. GEO and Europe PMC searches found no public CHO dataset with
glycan profiles and omics on the same clones at a usable n. The box is "requires partner data".

## What this work does not demonstrate

It does not demonstrate multi-omic fusion: every metric comes from a single modality, and the one
test of a shared pathway space gave a null on its pre-registered form. It does not demonstrate
prediction on BMS's own data, because the per-clone data behind Yao et al. 2021 are not public. It
does not demonstrate that the Δ branch can rank useful interventions: knockout recovery is at chance.
It does not demonstrate latent dynamics, glycan prediction or scale-risk prediction, none of which
could be tested. The two positive boxes rest on one 2011 microarray dataset from one group of labs,
with experiment and culture identifiers that I reconstructed. For viability, growth rate and
q-lactate, part or most of the signal is the temperature shift.
