# CHO proof of concept — Qp from transcriptomics on GSE30321

All numbers below are in `results/metrics.csv` and are regenerated by `python cho_poc.py --all`.
Intervals are 95% cluster-bootstrap intervals. RMSE is in pg/cell/day.

## Bottom line

1. **The published baseline is not beaten.** On the matched subset (stationary, temperature-shifted;
   75 arrays, 38 cultures), culture-grouped PLS gives RMSE **6.6 (5.3–7.9)** against the published
   4.44. The interval excludes 4.44.
2. **Expression predicts Qp of held-out cultures well: RMSE 5.8 (4.8–6.7), R² 0.83 (0.76–0.88),
   AUC 0.97 (0.93–1.00)** on all 167 labeled arrays. This is the number the brief asked for.
3. **Most of that is recognition of the experiment, not prediction for a new one.** With whole
   experiments held out, RMSE is **10.8 (8.1–13.3)**, R² 0.45 (0.21–0.62), AUC 0.92 (0.80–0.99).
   With whole families of related experiments held out, AUC is **0.71 (0.44–0.89)**, an interval
   that includes chance.
4. **Grouping by culture does not remove the leak in this dataset.** Array-level and culture-level
   splits give the same answer (RMSE 5.7 vs 6.0). The step is at the experiment level, because
   replicate flasks of one experiment are near-copies of each other.
5. **The pathway encoder loses to raw genes** (RMSE 10.1 vs 5.8 on held-out cultures). The array
   carries 15% of Hallmark genes, so this says little about a pathway encoder on a full transcriptome.
6. **The Δ decode recovers one of the three WGCNA themes cleanly** (vesicle transport), cell cycle
   weakly, protein secretion not.
7. **Self-supervised pretraining gives no reliable gain.** The average improvement over training
   from scratch (0.3–0.6 pg/cell/day) is inside seed-to-seed noise, and plain PLS beats both networks.

## Scope boundary

This work uses **one modality: transcriptomics**, on a 2011 custom microarray with 3,592 probesets.
It has **no proteomics, no metabolomics, no glycan measurements and no paired small/large-scale runs**.
It exercises three parts of the platform on that one modality: an encoder, a state representation
and the Δ decode. It does **not** test multi-omic fusion, latent dynamics or scale-up prediction,
and nothing here should be presented as evidence for them.

## 1. Data

| | |
|---|---|
| Arrays | 295, log2 RMA as deposited (no CEL files are public) |
| Probesets | 3,592 (GPL13791, WyeHamster2a) — not the ~20,000 assumed in the brief |
| Arrays with Qp | 169 as deposited, **167 used** (two removed by a label-free QC check, below) |
| Qp range | 0 to 55.4 pg/cell/day, mean 17.3, SD 13.9 |
| Labeled cultures / experiments | 62 cultures in 19 experiments |
| Share of Qp variance between experiments | 79% |
| Probesets with a gene symbol | 2,045 of 3,592 (56.9%), 1,707 unique symbols |
| Hallmark genes on the array | 653 of 4,384 (14.9%); 44 of 50 sets keep ≥10 genes |
| Feature counts | raw 1,000 probesets; pathway 44 scores; coexpression 40 modules |

**Qp labels** come verbatim from GEO sample characteristics. No fallback source was needed.

**Culture ID is derived, not deposited.** Neither GEO nor ArrayExpress carries a culture, cell-line
or time-point identifier. Arrays are deposited in contiguous experiment blocks in which replicate
cultures recur across time points with the same seeding density; that structure gives a culture ID
and a coarser experiment ID (`results/culture_map.csv`, rules in `CULTURE_RULES`). The derivation
yields **122 cultures against the 121 stated by GEO**, in 38 experiment blocks. Blocks that the
metadata cannot resolve contain no labeled array, with one exception (experiment E33, 8 arrays),
which is merged into a single group. A label-free check supports the map: for 93% of arrays the
most-correlated other array lies in the same derived experiment.

**Two arrays lost their label.** For arrays 248–251 the deposited metadata and the expression matrix
are offset by one position (the tight replicate triplet in expression is 249–251; the metadata says
248–250). Arrays 248 and 251 were removed from the labeled set before modelling. With them put back,
culture-grouped RMSE is 6.8 instead of 5.8; held-out-experiment RMSE is unchanged (10.9).

**Matched subset.** The paper's sample list is not public and growth phase is not annotated. The rule,
fixed before any model was run: temperature < 37 °C, growth rate ≤ 0.01 h⁻¹, Qp measured. That gives
75 arrays, 38 cultures, 12 experiments (the paper used 70 for model building).

## 2. Matched baseline comparison

This is the only comparison with the published 4.44. Model: PLS on the 1,000 highest-variance
probesets, components chosen inside each training fold.

| Hold-out unit | RMSE (95% CI) | R² (95% CI) | vs published 4.44 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arrays (leaky; ignores replicate structure) | 5.8 (4.6–6.9) | 0.77 (0.60–0.86) | worse |
| **Cultures, 5-fold — the head-to-head** | **6.6 (5.3–7.9)** | **0.69 (0.42–0.84)** | **worse** |
| Whole experiments | 11.1 (7.1–15.2) | 0.24 (−0.49–0.55) | worse |

No-skill reference on this subset (predict the training mean): RMSE 12.0.

Three caveats that cut in different directions:

- These are not the paper's samples. The subset is rebuilt from a description.
- The paper used jackknife gene selection (287 genes); this is plain PLS with no supervised selection.
  No variant was tried to close the gap, so that the number stays a single pre-specified test.
- The abstract does not say how replicate cultures were handled in the paper's cross-model validation.
  If replicates were split across folds, 4.44 is closest in kind to the array-level row (5.8), and
  shares its optimism.

Secondary rows, not head-to-head: elastic net on the same subset gives 6.8 (5.4–8.2); the model
trained on all labeled arrays, scored on the matched arrays only, gives 5.8 (4.3–7.3).

What the tables show:

- Model choice barely matters; the three regressors sit within each other's intervals.
- Expression carries far more than the batch record: temperature, growth rate, cell density and
  viability alone do barely better than predicting the mean (R² 0.08 on held-out cultures).
- Raw genes beat the pathway encoder on held-out cultures by a wide margin. On held-out experiments
  the gap narrows and the intervals overlap. Data-driven coexpression modules, fitted inside each
  fold, sit between the two.

## 4. Leakage comparison (`figures/fig2_leakage.png`)

Same model (PLS / logistic on raw genes), same 167 arrays, four hold-out units.

| What is held out together | RMSE (95% CI) | AUC (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|
| Arrays (leaky) | 5.7 (4.8–6.7) | 0.98 (0.96–1.00) |
| Cultures | 6.0 (5.0–7.1) | 0.98 (0.95–1.00) |
| Experiments (19) | 10.8 (8.1–13.3) | 0.92 (0.80–0.99) |
| Families of related experiments (9) | 10.3 (8.0–12.4) | 0.71 (0.44–0.89) |

The first two rows are means over 10 random partitions. Across partitions RMSE ranged 5.2–6.2
(arrays) and 5.3–6.9 (cultures), which is why the single-partition culture-grouped figure in
section 3 (5.8) differs slightly from the 6.0 here.

Grouping by culture satisfies the rule "no culture in both train and test" and is enforced by an
assertion on every split. It is not sufficient here. A culture is one flask; its sister flasks from
the same experiment have the same cell line, the same process and almost the same Qp, and they sit
in the training set. Holding out the experiment nearly doubles the error. Holding out families of
similar experiments leaves RMSE where it was but drops AUC to an interval that includes 0.5.

The families are a post hoc, unsupervised grouping (experiments whose mean expression profiles
correlate above 0.5; one family holds 6 of the 19 experiments and 65 of the 167 arrays). Treat that
row as a stress test, not as an estimate.

## 5. Δ decode (`results/delta_pathways.csv`)

Δ = mean pathway score in the top Qp quartile (≥ 25.3) minus the bottom quartile (≤ 5.9), in SD
units, over 167 arrays and 48 gene sets (44 Hallmark, plus 4 GO sets added for the WGCNA themes and
named before the decode was run). Each set is tested three ways: bootstrap over cultures and over
experiments; against 1,000 random gene sets of the same size; and again after removing every
experiment's mean from both the scores and Qp.

Top of the ranking:

| Rank | Gene set | Δ (95% CI) | p vs random sets | Holds within experiment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fatty acid metabolism | −1.59 (−1.95 to −1.11) | 0.003 | no |
| 2 | Adipogenesis | −1.57 (−1.91 to −1.05) | 0.002 | yes |
| 3 | Bile acid metabolism | −1.51 (−2.03 to −0.91) | 0.011 | no |
| 4 | Oxidative phosphorylation | −1.46 (−1.83 to −0.91) | 0.010 | no |
| 5 | **Golgi vesicle transport (GO)** | −1.43 (−1.98 to −0.75) | 0.010 | yes |
| 6 | Cholesterol homeostasis | −1.42 (−1.98 to −0.79) | 0.013 | yes |
| 7 | Coagulation | +1.38 (0.90 to 1.70) | 0.025 | no |
| 8 | **Mitotic spindle** | +1.22 (0.71 to 1.66) | 0.068 | no |

Eight of 48 sets beat random gene sets at p < 0.05, where 2.4 would be expected by chance.

**Do the WGCNA themes appear?**

| Theme | Gene set (genes on array) | Rank of 48 | Δ | p vs random | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vesicle transport | Golgi vesicle transport (42) | 5 | −1.43 | 0.010 | **Yes.** Survives all three tests. |
| Vesicle transport | Vesicle-mediated transport (171) | 29 | −0.78 | 0.13 | Weak overall; present within experiments. |
| Cell cycle | Mitotic spindle (28) | 8 | +1.22 | 0.068 | Suggestive. |
| Cell cycle | GO cell cycle (245) | 23 | +0.86 | 0.053 | Suggestive. |
| Cell cycle | E2F targets (52) | 28 | +0.79 | 0.22 | No. |
| Cell cycle | G2M checkpoint (42) | 33 | +0.59 | 0.40 | No. |
| Protein secretion | Hallmark protein secretion (27) | 22 | −0.90 | 0.22 | No. |
| Protein secretion | Protein localisation to extracellular region (51) | 27 | +0.79 | 0.23 | No. |

So: **vesicle transport is recovered, cell cycle is suggestive, protein secretion is not.** Driver
genes for Golgi vesicle transport are SEC22B, TMED4, RAB6A, LAMP1, LMAN2, AP3D1, RAB10 and TRAPPC3.

Two things a reviewer should know before reading biology into this:

- **The sign is negative.** High-Qp arrays score lower on Golgi vesicle transport and on lipid and
  mitochondrial metabolism. The WGCNA paper also reports modules negatively associated with Qp, so
  the direction is not a contradiction, but it is not "high producers have more secretory machinery".
- **The contrast is confounded.** The top quartile comes from 6 experiments, 5 of them in one
  expression family, and is 62% 37 °C arrays against 40% in the bottom quartile. The
  within-experiment variant removes cell-line identity but not culture phase or temperature.

## 6. Self-supervised pretraining (`figures/fig3_learning_curve.png`)

**Question.** Does masked-feature denoising pretraining on unlabeled CHO expression improve
small-sample Qp prediction, compared with the same network trained from scratch? No external weights.

**Answer: no reliable gain.** Pretraining inside the fold lowered test RMSE by 0.6 on average with
cultures held out and by 0.3 with experiments held out. Both are smaller than the seed-to-seed
spread, and the first is driven by one seed of five. Plain PLS beat both networks at every training
size when cultures were held out.

Design: encoder 3,592 → 256 → 32 with LayerNorm on `z`; 25% of features masked per sample; the
in-fold corpus is every array outside the held-out groups, labeled or not (244–263 arrays), with
labels never used; fine-tuning epochs chosen on a grouped split inside the training set; 5 seeds.
CPU only.

Test RMSE, mean over 5 seeds (SD in brackets):

| Held out | Training cultures | From scratch | Pretrained in fold (headline) | Pretrained, transductive | PLS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 cultures | 10 | 13.0 (2.8) | 11.8 (1.5) | 12.5 (1.8) | 10.4 (2.5) |
| | 20 | 9.8 (0.8) | 9.3 (1.1) | 9.4 (1.7) | 8.7 (1.3) |
| | 30 | 8.4 (1.2) | 7.6 (0.7) | 7.7 (1.2) | 7.1 (1.0) |
| | 40 | 7.1 (1.5) | 7.0 (1.2) | 6.9 (1.4) | 6.0 (1.4) |
| | 47 | 7.0 (1.5) | 6.7 (1.3) | 6.4 (1.4) | 5.5 (1.1) |
| 5 experiments | 10 | 13.2 (2.7) | 13.1 (2.9) | 14.8 (3.9) | 11.5 (3.8) |
| | 20 | 13.7 (2.5) | 12.8 (3.2) | 14.5 (3.8) | 13.1 (2.4) |
| | 30 | 12.4 (1.6) | 12.1 (2.4) | 13.1 (3.2) | 11.9 (1.3) |
| | 40 | 11.8 (1.8) | 12.0 (2.6) | 13.1 (3.5) | 12.0 (1.5) |

Paired comparison, pretrained in fold minus from scratch (same seed, same train and test arrays):

| Held out | Mean difference over all sizes | Runs where pretraining was better | Per-seed mean difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 cultures | −0.61 | 17 of 25 | −0.21, −0.01, +0.05, −0.64, −2.21 |
| 5 experiments | −0.29 | 11 of 20 | +0.23, −1.97, +0.82, −0.41, −0.10 |

Other observations:

- The transductive arm, which sees the test arrays' expression during pretraining, is no better
  than the in-fold arm, and is worse when experiments are held out. It did not behave as an upper bound.
- A frozen in-fold encoder with ridge regression on `z` (linear probe) matched training from scratch
  on held-out cultures (+0.14) and was 0.8 better on held-out experiments, winning 15 of 20 runs.
  That is the only hint of a useful representation, and it is not the pre-specified headline arm.
- With whole experiments held out, no method improves with more training cultures. More flasks of
  the same experiments do not help; more experiments would.

**This protocol was revised once after seeing results, and both runs are reported.** The first
version had no normalisation on `z` and weak regularisation. Pretraining made test error worse at
every size (17.9 vs 12.4 at 10 cultures) and the transductive arm was worse than predicting the
mean. Training-set diagnostics showed both networks memorising the training set within 40 epochs and
the pretrained encoder emitting activations 40 times larger than a fresh one, so that run measured an
unstable fine-tune. The fix (LayerNorm on `z`, input dropout, stronger weight decay, applied to
every arm) was committed to as final before its results existed. First-run files are in
`results/superseded/`.

## What this work does not demonstrate

It does not demonstrate multi-omic fusion, latent dynamics, or prediction across scales; none of
the three was tested, and the data to test them (proteomics, metabolomics, glycans, paired small-
and large-scale runs) are not in this dataset. It does not demonstrate prediction of Qp for a new
cell line: the strict splits give an error of about 11 pg/cell/day and intervals that reach chance.
It does not improve on the 2011 published model. The encoder tested is a fixed pathway average on an
array that covers a seventh of the pathway genes, so it is weak evidence about the platform's
trained, pathway-constrained encoder in either direction. The Δ decode was run on pathway scores,
not on a learned culture state, and its one clean hit is an association in a contrast confounded by
cell line and temperature. Every interval here treats cultures or experiments as the independent
unit, yet the cultures are my reconstruction from deposit order and seeding density, not the
authors' own identifiers.
